What a page can see

Five ways a tab
tells on you.

Each one is a separate signal. Focus Spoofer answers all of them on the tabs you turn it on for, and leaves the rest of the page alone, so forms, menus and video controls still behave.

01

The tab switch

document.hidden
What the page does

Listens for visibilitychange and reads document.hidden the moment you open another tab.

What it sees instead

Always visible. The event never reaches the page, and even prototype-level checks get the same answer.

09:14:02  play       lecture.mp4
09:14:09  visibilitychange → hidden
09:14:09  pause      "Are you still there?"
09:14:09  document.hidden = false
09:21:40  ended      100% watched
02

The other window

document.hasFocus()
What the page does

Watches for blur on the window, or polls hasFocus(), to catch you in another app or on a second monitor.

What it sees instead

Focus never leaves. Clicking between fields inside the page still fires normal focus and blur, so validation keeps working.

03

The stalled clock

requestAnimationFrame
What the page does

A hidden tab stops painting, so animation callbacks freeze. Some sites time the gap and conclude you left, with no event at all.

What it sees instead

Callbacks keep arriving at a steady cadence while the tab is hidden. When it's visible, the browser drives them natively and nothing extra runs.

frame interval while you're away
04

The exit

mouseleave
What the page does

Notices the pointer leaving the page, or pagehide firing, and treats it as you walking away.

What it sees instead

Nothing at page level. Hover menus inside the page still open and close on their own mouseleave.

nav .menu      mouseleave → close
document       mouseleave → "idle"
window         pagehide
session        still active
05

The fingerprint

canvas.toDataURL()
What the page does

Draws hidden text and shapes, reads the pixels back and hashes them into an ID that follows your device around.

What it sees instead

Invisible noise in canvas, WebGL and audio readback, different on every load. You can switch this part off in Settings.

The interface

One switch.
That's the interface.

Turn it on for the tab you're on, or keep a site protected on every visit. Everything else lives on one quiet settings page.

Straight answers

What it won't do.

Beat proctoring or anti-cheat.

Exam and anti-cheat software watch far more than a web page can. We don't try to get around them, and you shouldn't rely on this for that.

Run everywhere.

It works on regular web pages in Chrome, Brave and Edge. Browser pages like chrome://, the Web Store and the built-in PDF viewer are off limits to every extension.

Collect your browsing.

No account, no history, no sites. Usage stats are off unless you opt in, and even then they're daily counts with no ID. Privacy policy.
